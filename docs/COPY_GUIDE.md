# Copy guide

## Voice

KeenKids sounds like a thoughtful teacher talking to a parent at pickup. Warm, specific, unhurried, a little playful. It never sounds like a brochure.

- **Say what kids do.** Write "Kids build, test, change their minds and try again." Don't write "an interdisciplinary growth mindset."
- **Use plain words, short paragraphs and varied sentence lengths.** One light line per section is plenty, for example "October gets a little spooky."
- **Answer parents' real questions:** what my child does, whether they'll enjoy it, whether they're safe, where and when, what it costs, how to sign up.
- **Use American spelling** (favorite, color, enroll). Use real punctuation: curly quotes and apostrophes, en dashes in ranges (grades 1–5, 2:30–5:30 PM).

## Words we don't use

Unlock, empower, transformative, holistic, world-class, journey, limitless potential, innovation meets creativity, spark, revolutionize, tomorrow's leaders, cutting-edge, nurture (in headings), "spots are filling fast".

## Headings

- A heading is a sentence a person would say: "Where does your child go to school?", "What it costs", "Questions parents ask".
- No eyebrow labels above headings, and no section numbers.
- Don't build a section as a vague heading followed by three buzzwords.

## Calls to action

- Name the action and the destination: "Open the Mattos enrollment form", "Join the Curtner interest list", "Write the email".
- Interest forms are called interest forms. Enrollment forms are called enrollment forms.
- The primary action is always **Find your school**. The secondary action is **Ask us a question** or **Explore the program**.

## Truth rules

- Every fact must trace to `docs/CONTENT_INVENTORY.md` or a school's own form.
- Testimonials are verbatim, with the attribution as published.
- The founders' story and the FAQ answers stay word for word wherever they are shown as the official text. Summaries (homepage founders, policy notes) keep the facts unchanged.
- Never invent capacity, availability, deadlines, discounts, ratings, credentials, safety guarantees or "most popular".
- Where the source disagrees with itself, say what each source says. For example: "Listed for November 18. The form says the exact date will be confirmed."
- Confirmation messages say exactly what happened. Example: "Your email app should have opened… It isn't sent until you press send there."

## Microcopy

- **Errors** say what to do: "Please tell us your name." "A sentence or two helps us answer properly."
- **Privacy hint** on message fields: "Please don't include your child's full name or anything private."
- **External links** have visible arrow icons, plus screen-reader text such as "(opens Google Forms in a new tab)".
